# Bonus 2 — Synthetic Media Governance Enforcement and Audit Mechanism

## 1. Purpose

A policy without a way to verify compliance risks becoming a statement of intent rather than an operational control.

This audit framework defines how the University Communications Office can monitor compliance with its Synthetic Media Governance Policy.

The objective is not to create a surveillance-heavy process. It is to make important controls measurable, identify failures early, and distinguish genuine compliance from documentation that only appears complete.

---

## 2. Governance Ownership

### Policy Owner

**Director of University Communications**

Responsible for:

- policy maintenance;
- approval of high-risk governance decisions;
- annual review;
- incident escalation.

### Governance Coordinator

A designated communications operations or AI governance employee should maintain the synthetic-media project register and audit evidence.

### Project Owner

Responsible for:

- accurate project classification;
- source content;
- consent;
- vendor selection;
- disclosure;
- maintaining required records.

### Communications Approver

Responsible for confirming that the project satisfies the appropriate risk-tier requirements before distribution.

---

# 3. Required Project Register

All Tier 2 and Tier 3 projects should be entered into a central register containing:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Project ID | Unique audit reference |
| Request Owner | Accountability |
| Project description | Purpose |
| Risk tier | Required control level |
| AI tool/vendor | Vendor governance |
| Real-person likeness? | Consent trigger |
| Consent status | Identity authorization |
| Disclosure method | Transparency |
| Provenance available? | Provenance |
| Approval status | Governance |
| Distribution channels | Scope |
| Expiration/review date | Lifecycle control |
| Incident status | Response tracking |

Tier 1 projects may use a simplified record.

---

# 4. Control Metrics

## Consent

**Metric:**

> Percentage of Tier 3 projects with documented consent before generation.

**Target:** 100%

**Evidence:**

- signed consent record;
- approved project ID;
- scope and expiration date.

A project with consent obtained after production counts as a control failure.

---

## Disclosure

**Metric:**

> Percentage of public synthetic-media artifacts with required disclosure at publication.

**Target:** 100%

**Evidence:**

- final published artifact;
- screenshot or publication record;
- disclosure wording.

---

## Human Approval

**Metric:**

> Percentage of Tier 3 projects with completed second-review approval before publication.

**Target:** 100%

---

## Approved Tools

**Metric:**

> Percentage of Tier 2 and Tier 3 projects using an approved AI vendor.

**Target:** 100%

---

## Provenance / Records

**Metric:**

> Percentage of applicable projects retaining the required source, generation, consent, and approval records.

**Target:** 100%

---

## Incident Reporting

**Metric:**

> Percentage of identified synthetic-media incidents documented in the incident register.

**Target:** 100%

This does not mean the organization will have zero incidents. It measures whether known incidents are being recorded and investigated.

---

# 5. Audit Schedule

## Quarterly Sample Audit

Every quarter, the Governance Coordinator should randomly sample:

- at least three Tier 2 projects;
- every Tier 3 project created during the period, where practical;
- at least one completed or expired project.

The audit should verify documentation against the final public artifact.

## Annual Full Review

Once per year, the Policy Owner should review:

- the policy itself;
- the approved-tool list;
- incident trends;
- consent and reuse practices;
- platform changes;
- applicable legal requirements;
- employee training;
- audit findings.

---

# 6. What the Audit Should Actually Test

The audit should not only ask:

> "Is there a form?"

It should compare the record against the artifact.

For example:

### Consent test

Does the consent record authorize the actual person, purpose, audience, and reuse shown in the final artifact?

### Disclosure test

Can a reasonable viewer see or hear the required disclosure in the actual published version?

### Approval test

Did the approved script match the final media?

### Vendor test

Was the final artifact actually generated using the approved platform recorded in the project register?

### Change-control test

Was material post-approval editing documented?

### Expiration test

Is an expired synthetic likeness still being used?

---

# 7. Exception and Escalation Process

A failed control should be assigned one of three statuses:

### Minor

Example:

- missing documentation that can be reconstructed;
- non-material metadata issue.

Action:

Correct record within a defined period.

### Significant

Example:

- missing required disclosure;
- missing second approval;
- use of a tool without prior approval.

Action:

Pause further distribution, assess impact, remediate, and document.

### Critical

Example:

- non-consensual likeness;
- false executive statement;
- unauthorized high-impact communication;
- synthetic media distributed in a safety or financial context.

Action:

Immediate containment and escalation to appropriate Communications, Legal, Privacy, Security, or leadership functions.

---

# 8. Incident and Complaint Mechanism

The organization should provide a clear way for employees and affected individuals to report:

- unauthorized synthetic likeness use;
- missing disclosure;
- false attribution;
- misleading recontextualization;
- suspected misuse of an AI platform;
- or other policy violations.

Reports should receive:

1. acknowledgement;
2. triage;
3. risk classification;
4. documented disposition;
5. corrective action when necessary.

The organization should not require a complainant to prove that content is synthetic before opening an investigation.

---

# 9. Audit Trail Retention

The organization should retain project records for a period appropriate to the communication's sensitivity and applicable retention requirements.

At minimum, the record should allow an auditor to reconstruct:

**who requested → what was generated → which tool was used → whose likeness was involved → what consent existed → who approved → what was published → where it was distributed → whether an incident occurred.**

Retention periods should be reviewed with the organization's legal, privacy, and records-management requirements rather than set solely for convenience.

---

# 10. Training and Compliance

Employees responsible for synthetic media should complete role-appropriate training covering:

- consent;
- disclosure;
- approved AI tools;
- prohibited uses;
- high-risk communications;
- incident reporting;
- and basic synthetic-media limitations.

Training completion should be measurable.

A useful metric is:

> Percentage of employees authorized to request Tier 2/Tier 3 synthetic media who have completed required training.

---

# 11. Sunset and Review Triggers

The policy should be reviewed earlier than its normal annual cycle when:

- a major AI vendor changes its terms or capabilities;
- a material incident occurs;
- a new legal requirement takes effect;
- the organization begins a materially different synthetic-media use case;
- provenance or disclosure standards materially change;
- or an audit identifies recurring control failures.

---

# 12. What Should Not Be Used as a KPI

The following metrics can be misleading if treated as proof of responsible governance:

- number of AI videos created;
- percentage of employees using AI tools;
- number of disclosures alone;
- number of detector scans;
- total training hours.

These measure activity, not whether the organization is actually reducing risk.

A better audit program measures whether required controls **worked when tested against real projects**.

---

# 13. Minimum Governance Dashboard

A quarterly dashboard could report:

| Measure | Target |
|---|---:|
| Tier 3 projects with valid consent | 100% |
| Public artifacts with required disclosure | 100% |
| Tier 3 projects with second approval | 100% |
| Projects using approved vendors | 100% |
| Projects with complete audit records | 100% |
| Known incidents documented | 100% |
| Required employee training completion | 100% |

Any failure should be accompanied by an explanation and remediation status rather than hidden by averaging.

---

# 14. Final Governance Principle

The purpose of auditing is not to prove that synthetic-media risk is zero.

It is to verify that:

- high-risk uses were identified;
- required safeguards were actually applied;
- exceptions were visible;
- incidents were handled;
- and the organization learned when controls failed.

A governance program becomes meaningful when a reviewer can reconstruct what happened to a synthetic-media artifact after the fact and identify who was responsible for each major decision.

## A New Policy for Synthetic Media

The University Communications Office is adopting a new policy governing the use of AI-generated and AI-manipulated audio, video, imagery, avatars, and voices.

The goal is not to prevent responsible experimentation with AI.

The goal is to make sure that when we use synthetic media, we know:

- why we are using it;
- whose identity is involved;
- whether we have permission;
- what our audience is being told;
- who reviewed the content;
- and what happens if something goes wrong.

Synthetic-media tools are becoming easy to use. That accessibility creates useful opportunities for accessibility, translation, training, creative production, and communications. It also creates new risks involving identity, misinformation, privacy, and institutional trust.

This policy establishes a common process for managing those risks.

---

# What Is Changing?

## 1. We are introducing risk tiers

Synthetic-media projects will be classified according to risk.

Low-risk educational or prototype uses may require only basic review.

Projects involving real people's likenesses, public-facing communications, or higher-consequence topics will require additional approval.

Some uses—such as deceptive impersonation, non-consensual likeness use, and certain high-impact official communications—are prohibited.

---

## 2. Consent is required for real-person likenesses

Do not upload a person's photograph, voice recording, or other identity-bearing material to an AI tool for synthetic reproduction unless the required authorization has been obtained.

A public photograph is not automatically permission to create an AI avatar.

Permission to use someone in one project does not automatically authorize future reuse.

---

## 3. Use approved AI tools

Do not use an unapproved consumer AI service for university synthetic-media work simply because it is faster or free.

If the capability you need is not available through an approved tool, contact the Communications Office for review rather than bypassing the process.

---

## 4. Label public synthetic media

When a reasonable viewer could mistake AI-generated media for authentic human-created media, required disclosure must be included.

Examples:

> **AI-Generated Synthetic Media**

For a real-person avatar, more specific language may be appropriate:

> **AI-generated avatar. The person depicted did not record this video.**

A platform's automatic label should not be treated as our only safeguard for higher-risk content.

---

## 5. High-impact communications need higher scrutiny

Do not use synthetic representations for emergency, safety, financial, legal, disciplinary, or other high-consequence communications unless the policy explicitly permits the use.

When a real person's personal authority is important to the communication, an authentic human communicator should generally be preferred.

---

# What You Need to Do Before Starting a Project

Before generating synthetic media, ask:

### 1. Why are we using synthetic media?

Can the communication purpose be clearly explained?

### 2. Is anyone's identity being reproduced?

If yes, confirm documented consent before production.

### 3. What will the audience see?

Determine whether the content could reasonably be mistaken for authentic human communication.

### 4. Is the AI tool approved?

Do not upload identity-bearing source material to an unapproved platform.

### 5. Who is accountable?

Every project needs a Request Owner and, where required, an approver.

---

# What to Do When You Are Unsure

Do not generate first and ask for approval later.

Contact the designated Communications reviewer when:

- the use case is unusual;
- a real person's voice or likeness is involved;
- the audience is large or public;
- the message concerns a sensitive topic;
- you are unsure whether a disclosure is sufficient;
- or you cannot determine the appropriate risk tier.

A request can be modified, escalated, or declined. Asking early is part of the process.

---

# Why We Are Doing This

Our objective is not to treat AI tools as inherently good or bad.

We want to make responsible use easier and risky use harder.

A synthetic-media tool may be able to generate a convincing artifact quickly. That does not mean that the organization should publish everything it can generate.

The standard is:

**legitimate purpose + truthful content + appropriate consent + clear disclosure + approved technology + accountable human review.**

Where those conditions cannot be met, we should use another communication method or decline the synthetic-media request.

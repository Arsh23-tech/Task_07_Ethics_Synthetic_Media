# Phase B — Synthetic Media Governance Policy
## University Communications Office

**Policy Status:** Proposed  
**Applies To:** University Communications Office employees, student employees, contractors, interns, and approved vendors acting on behalf of the office  
**Policy Owner:** Director of University Communications  
**Review Cycle:** At least annually and whenever material changes occur to applicable law, platform requirements, or approved AI tools

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes operational requirements for the creation, review, approval, distribution, storage, and retirement of synthetic audio, video, imagery, avatars, and voice representations used by the University Communications Office.

The policy is designed to allow legitimate uses of synthetic media while protecting:
- individual consent and control over identity;
- the accuracy and integrity of university communications;
- privacy and security of source images, recordings, and voice data;
- institutional reputation and public trust;
- accessibility and audience understanding; and
- the University's ability to identify, investigate, and correct misuse.

The policy treats synthetic media as a communications capability requiring human accountability. The use of an AI generation tool does not transfer responsibility for the resulting communication to the tool provider.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to synthetic media created, commissioned, edited, or distributed by the University Communications Office for:
- university websites;
- social media;
- marketing and recruitment;
- admissions communications;
- alumni and donor communications;
- internal communications;
- employee or student training;
- educational demonstrations;
- events and presentations;
- digital signage;
- public relations;
- promotional campaigns; and
- other external or internal communications distributed under the University's name.

It applies whether content is generated internally or by an external provider.

This policy also applies when a staff member uses a personal account or personally owned device to create content for official university communication.

Personal, non-work use by employees is outside this policy unless the activity uses university data, represents the University, or could reasonably be interpreted as an official university communication.

---

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Synthetic Media
Audio, video, imagery, avatars, voices, or other digital media that are generated or materially altered using AI.

### 3.2 Synthetic Likeness
An AI-generated representation of a person's face, voice, body, mannerisms, or other identifiable characteristics.

### 3.3 Public-Facing Content
Content distributed outside the University or made available to an audience that may include individuals who are not part of the originating project team.

### 3.4 High-Impact Communication
A communication that could reasonably affect safety, finances, legal rights, employment or student status, institutional reputation, markets, public policy, or other consequential decisions.

### 3.5 Provenance
Information about the origin, creation, modification, and handling of a digital asset.

### 3.6 Request Owner
The employee responsible for the purpose, factual accuracy, source materials, and intended distribution of a synthetic-media project.

### 3.7 Communications Approver
The person authorized by the Director of Communications to approve synthetic media for the applicable risk tier.

---

## 4. Governing Principles

All synthetic-media projects must follow these principles:

1. **Truth before realism.** Verify the underlying claims before optimizing how believable the presentation is.
2. **Consent before representation.** A real person's likeness or voice may not be synthetically reproduced without documented authorization.
3. **Disclosure before exposure.** Public audiences should not have to infer that synthetic media was used.
4. **Human accountability.** Every project must have an identifiable Request Owner and reviewer.
5. **Minimum necessary data.** Do not upload more personal image, audio, or identity data than is necessary.
6. **Higher risk requires stronger controls.** High-impact uses require additional review or are prohibited.
7. **Refusal is an available outcome.** The office will decline requests that cannot be made responsible through safeguards.
8. **Reuse is not automatic.** Permission to create one artifact does not create unlimited permission to reuse a person's likeness or voice.

---

## 5. Risk Classification and Approval Matrix

Every proposed project must be assigned a risk tier before generation.

| Tier | Typical Use | Approval | Synthetic Real-Person Likeness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Low** | Fictional avatar, clearly labeled educational demonstration, internal prototype, synthetic narration of already-approved content | Request Owner + basic communications check | Generally not required |
| **Tier 2 — Moderate** | Public-facing synthetic narration, translation, accessibility content, marketing/training with no real-person impersonation | Request Owner + Communications Approver | Only with documented consent |
| **Tier 3 — High** | Public content using a real person's likeness/voice; executive or senior-leader avatar; recruitment or donor communications using a recognizable person | Written consent + Communications Approver + Privacy/Legal review when applicable | Required |
| **Tier 4 — Critical / Prohibited** | Emergency alerts, safety instructions, financial or legal statements, disciplinary decisions, fabricated official statements, non-consensual likenesses, deceptive impersonation | **Do not use synthetic representation** | Not permitted |

The Communications Office may assign a higher tier when facts are uncertain.

---

## 6. Permitted Uses

Synthetic media may be used when the purpose is legitimate and the project passes the applicable approval requirements.

### 6.1 Educational and Research Demonstrations
Synthetic avatars and voices may be used to demonstrate AI capabilities in classes, research, workshops, conferences, and internal training.

### 6.2 Accessibility and Translation
Synthetic narration may be used to create accessibility-oriented or translated versions of content that has already been approved. The original source content remains authoritative.

### 6.3 Fictional or Generic Presenters
Fictional presenters, generic avatars, and synthetic characters may be used when the audience is not led to believe that a real person participated.

### 6.4 Approved Marketing and Recruitment
Synthetic media may be used when the content is accurate, no person is represented without consent, required disclosure is included, and the project passes the appropriate approval tier.

### 6.5 Internal Training and Prototyping
Synthetic media may be used internally to prototype scripts, storyboards, training experiences, or communications concepts. Drafts must not be published accidentally.

---

## 7. Prohibited Uses

The following uses are prohibited regardless of claimed business benefit, urgency, humor, or disclosure.

### 7.1 Non-Consensual Identity Reproduction
Do not create a synthetic likeness or voice of a student, faculty member, employee, administrator, donor, applicant, public figure, or other identifiable person without documented authorization.

### 7.2 False Attribution
Do not make a real person appear to have said, endorsed, approved, performed, or participated in something they did not actually say, endorse, approve, perform, or participate in.

### 7.3 High-Impact Synthetic Statements
Do not use synthetic representations of people for:
- emergency alerts;
- public safety instructions;
- legal notices;
- disciplinary decisions;
- employment decisions;
- financial disclosures or investment-related statements;
- crisis statements requiring personal accountability; or
- official statements in which the represented person's authenticity is material to the message.

If synthetic production assistance is useful for drafting, the final public statement must come from an appropriately authorized human communicator.

### 7.4 Deceptive Context
Do not remove, conceal, or intentionally weaken required AI disclosure to make synthetic content appear authentic.

### 7.5 Political or Advocacy Endorsement by Simulation
Do not use a synthetic likeness or voice to make a real person appear to endorse a political candidate, campaign, lobbying position, advocacy position, or political message that the person did not actually authorize.

### 7.6 Circumventing a Refusal
Do not submit a rejected project to another unapproved AI service to bypass this policy.

### 7.7 Unauthorized Reuse
Do not reuse an existing voice model, avatar, or other synthetic identity representation outside the purpose, audience, duration, and channels covered by the original authorization.

---

## 8. Consent Workflow for Real People

When a real person's recognizable likeness or voice will be reproduced, the Request Owner must obtain documented consent before generation.

The consent record must state:
1. The person's identity.
2. The image, recording, voice, or likeness being used.
3. The purpose of the synthetic representation.
4. The content types it may produce.
5. Intended audience and distribution channels.
6. Start date and expiration date.
7. Whether future reuse is permitted.
8. Whether the representation may be edited or generated into new messages.
9. Whether third-party AI vendors will process the source material.
10. Whether compensation is provided.
11. How the person can revoke permission.
12. What happens to stored source material and models after expiration or revocation.

### Consent rules
- Consent for one project does not automatically authorize unrelated projects.
- Consent to use a photograph does not automatically constitute consent to clone a voice or create a reusable avatar.
- Consent must be obtained before production.
- A revocation request must trigger a review of active campaigns and future reuse.
- For Tier 3 projects, consent must be stored in the University's designated recordkeeping system.

---

## 9. Third-Party AI Vendor and Data Requirements

Before uploading a person's photograph, voice recording, video, or other identity-bearing information to an external AI platform, the tool must be on the University's approved-tool list for that use case.

The Request Owner must confirm, through the applicable procurement, privacy, security, or legal process, that:
- the use is authorized;
- the vendor's terms are acceptable for the intended data;
- retention and deletion practices are understood;
- the content is not submitted in violation of consent;
- the organization understands whether uploaded material may be used to improve models or services;
- access to source identity material is appropriately controlled.

Sensitive source materials must not be uploaded to an unapproved consumer AI service merely because it is free or convenient.

---

## 10. Disclosure Standards

Public-facing synthetic media must include a disclosure whenever a reasonable viewer could otherwise mistake the content for authentic human-created media.

### Minimum disclosure

Preferred wording:

> **AI-Generated Synthetic Media**

When a real person is represented, a more specific disclosure should be used when appropriate:

> **AI-generated avatar. The person depicted did not record this video.**

or

> **AI-generated voice used with the speaker's authorization.**

### Placement

The disclosure should be:
- visible or audible;
- understandable without specialized technical knowledge;
- presented at or before first exposure where practical;
- retained when content is excerpted or republished where reasonably possible.

A platform-generated AI label should not be the University's only disclosure mechanism for high-risk public content.

---

## 11. Provenance and Content Credentials

Where the approved generation or publishing platform supports provenance information or C2PA Content Credentials, the Communications Office should preserve that information through the publishing workflow where technically feasible.

For each approved public synthetic artifact, the project record should retain:
- original source file;
- final published file;
- generation platform;
- model/tool information when available;
- creation date;
- material editing steps;
- consent record where applicable;
- approval record;
- disclosure method;
- distribution channels.

Provenance is treated as evidence about the asset's origin and history, not as proof that the underlying claims are truthful.

If provenance is lost during a workflow, the artifact does not automatically become prohibited; however, the Request Owner must record the loss and ensure that required disclosure remains available through another mechanism.

---

## 12. Human Review and Approval Workflow

Every public synthetic-media project must have a Request Owner and an identified reviewer.

### Tier 1
The Request Owner verifies:
- source accuracy;
- appropriate disclosure;
- absence of unauthorized real-person likenesses.

### Tier 2
A Communications Approver additionally reviews:
- audience;
- distribution context;
- disclosure;
- factual accuracy;
- potential reputational concerns.

### Tier 3
The project additionally requires:
- documented consent;
- Communications Approver approval;
- Privacy/Legal review when applicable;
- verification of the approved AI vendor;
- confirmation of provenance/disclosure procedures.

### Tier 4
The synthetic-media proposal is refused when prohibited-use rules apply.

---

## 13. Pre-Publication Checklist

Before public release, the Request Owner must confirm:

- [ ] The underlying content has been fact-checked.
- [ ] The intended use is permitted under this policy.
- [ ] Any real person's likeness or voice has documented consent.
- [ ] The AI platform is approved for the data being processed.
- [ ] Required disclosure is present.
- [ ] Provenance information has been preserved where supported.
- [ ] The final media matches the approved script/content.
- [ ] No unauthorized edits were introduced after approval.
- [ ] Distribution channels match the approved scope.
- [ ] The artifact and approval record have been archived.

Tier 3 projects require a second reviewer to complete the checklist.

---

## 14. Change Control and Reuse

Any material change after approval requires reassessment.

Material changes include:
- new spokesperson or avatar;
- new voice;
- substantially different message;
- new target audience;
- new distribution channel;
- new country or legal jurisdiction;
- new third-party AI platform;
- extension beyond the original expiration date;
- use of the asset for a high-impact communication.

A previously approved artifact may not be treated as permanently approved.

---

## 15. Incident Response

Synthetic-media incidents must be reported to the Communications Office as soon as they are identified.

Examples include:
- unauthorized use of a person's likeness;
- missing or removed disclosure;
- false or materially incorrect content;
- distribution outside the approved audience;
- use of an unapproved AI platform;
- compromised voice or avatar credentials;
- misleading recontextualization of an approved artifact.

### Response process

**1. Contain:** Pause scheduled distribution and remove university-controlled copies where possible.

**2. Preserve:** Retain the original artifact, approval record, generation record, and available distribution evidence.

**3. Assess:** Determine what was generated, who was represented, where it was distributed, and whether any person or organization was materially affected.

**4. Notify:** Notify the Communications Director and the relevant privacy, legal, information-security, or leadership contact based on the incident.

**5. Correct:** Issue a correction, clarification, or withdrawal when necessary.

**6. Review:** Record the root cause and determine whether a workflow, tool, training, or policy change is needed.

---

## 16. Audit and Compliance

The Communications Office should maintain a register of Tier 2 and Tier 3 synthetic-media projects.

At minimum, the register should record:
- project owner;
- date created;
- tool used;
- artifact type;
- risk tier;
- represented person, if any;
- consent status;
- approval status;
- disclosure method;
- distribution channel;
- expiration/review date;
- incident status, if applicable.

The Office should conduct periodic sampling of approved public artifacts.

Suggested compliance measures:
- 100% of Tier 3 projects have documented consent.
- 100% of public synthetic artifacts have required disclosure.
- 100% of Tier 3 projects have documented human approval.
- 100% of approved external AI tools are recorded on the approved-tool list.
- Any material incident is documented and reviewed.

Metrics are intended to identify control failures; they are not a guarantee that misuse will not occur.

---

## 17. Exceptions

Exceptions may not override the prohibited-use section.

For uses not clearly covered by the policy, the Request Owner must submit the project for review before generation or publication.

The reviewer may:
- approve;
- approve with additional safeguards;
- require a different production method;
- escalate to Privacy/Legal/Security; or
- refuse the request.

When uncertainty exists about whether a project is high impact, it should be treated as the higher-risk tier until reviewed.

---

## 18. Refusal Standard

The Communications Office must refuse a synthetic-media request when:
- required consent cannot be established;
- the request intentionally creates false attribution;
- the synthetic representation is being used to communicate a high-impact official statement;
- required disclosure would be hidden, misleading, or impractical;
- the requester is attempting to bypass a previous refusal;
- approved-vendor requirements cannot be satisfied;
- the organization cannot identify a responsible Request Owner; or
- the project creates a level of identity or reputational risk that cannot reasonably be controlled.

A legitimate business or communications purpose does not override these conditions.

---

## 19. Policy Limitations and Residual Risk

This policy cannot guarantee that synthetic media will remain attached to its original context after publication.

A third party may crop, screen-record, translate, edit, or repost an artifact. A disclosure may disappear. Provenance information may be unavailable to a downstream viewer. Automated detection may produce uncertain results. Employees may misunderstand or deliberately circumvent the policy.

The policy also cannot guarantee that a consented synthetic representation will never be used outside its intended context.

Its purpose is therefore risk reduction and accountability, not perfect prevention.

The organization should assume that some synthetic content will eventually leave its original environment and design workflows accordingly.

---

## 20. Industry Transferability

Although this policy is written for a University Communications Office, its control structure is transferable to corporate communications, marketing, HR, customer education, executive communications, recruiting, and training.

The context-specific decisions should change by industry, but the core controls remain useful:
- classify risk before generation;
- obtain documented consent for real-person likenesses;
- control third-party AI vendors;
- disclose synthetic content;
- preserve provenance where possible;
- require human review for consequential communications;
- maintain an auditable project record;
- define explicit prohibited uses; and
- provide a clear refusal path.

For a corporate setting, the same framework could be adapted to executive communications, customer-facing campaigns, product demonstrations, employee training, recruiting, investor relations, or customer-service content. High-impact financial, legal, safety, and executive statements should continue to receive heightened scrutiny or use an authentic human speaker rather than a synthetic representation.

---

## 21. Policy Principle

The Communications Office may use synthetic media when there is:

**a legitimate purpose + truthful content + appropriate consent + clear disclosure + approved technology + accountable human review.**

When those conditions cannot be established, the organization should use a non-synthetic communication method or refuse the project.

# Bonus 1 — Corporate Synthetic Media Governance Policy
## Corporate Communications & Marketing Function

**Policy Status:** Proposed  
**Applies To:** Corporate Communications, Marketing, Brand, HR Communications, Learning & Development, approved agencies, and contractors acting on behalf of the organization  
**Policy Owner:** Chief Communications Officer or designated AI Governance Owner  
**Review Cycle:** At least annually and when material changes occur to law, approved AI tools, or platform requirements

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs the responsible use of synthetic audio, video, imagery, avatars, and voice representations in corporate communications.

The organization may use synthetic media to improve accessibility, localization, training, creative production, and communication efficiency. It must not use synthetic representation in ways that create deceptive attribution, misuse a person's identity, or materially mislead customers, employees, investors, partners, or the public.

---

## 2. Risk Tiers

| Tier | Example | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 — Low | Fictional avatar for training prototype; synthetic narration of approved content | Project owner + communications check |
| Tier 2 — Moderate | Marketing localization, accessibility narration, internal training | Project owner + Communications reviewer |
| Tier 3 — High | Executive avatar/voice, customer-facing campaign using a real employee's likeness, major recruitment campaign | Written consent + senior Communications approval; Privacy/Legal review where applicable |
| Tier 4 — Critical / Prohibited | Investor/financial statements, crisis or safety announcements, deceptive executive impersonation, non-consensual likeness | Synthetic representation prohibited |

---

## 3. Permitted Uses

Examples include:

- localization and translation of approved marketing content;
- accessibility-oriented narration;
- internal training;
- fictional or generic presenters;
- product demonstrations that do not falsely represent a real person;
- approved marketing content using a real person's likeness with documented consent;
- prototyping and storyboarding.

---

## 4. Prohibited Uses

The organization must not:

- create a synthetic likeness or voice without documented authorization;
- make an executive, employee, customer, or partner appear to say or endorse something they did not;
- use synthetic representation for material financial or investor communications;
- fabricate crisis, safety, legal, or regulatory communications;
- hide required synthetic-media disclosure;
- reuse a person's voice or avatar beyond the agreed purpose or term;
- use an unapproved AI tool to bypass governance controls;
- create deceptive political, lobbying, or advocacy endorsements by simulation.

---

## 5. Consent

For any recognizable real-person likeness or voice, documented consent must specify:

- purpose;
- audience;
- distribution channels;
- duration;
- reuse rights;
- whether new messages may be generated;
- approved vendors;
- revocation process; and
- storage/deletion expectations.

Permission to use a photograph, recording, or prior video does not automatically mean permission to create a reusable synthetic likeness.

---

## 6. Vendor and Data Controls

Only approved AI vendors may process company or identity-bearing source material.

Before upload, the project owner must confirm:

- the use is authorized;
- the vendor is approved;
- retention and deletion practices are understood;
- identity data is being used only for the authorized purpose;
- the project does not expose restricted or sensitive information.

---

## 7. Disclosure

Public-facing synthetic media must include a clear disclosure whenever a reasonable viewer could mistake it for authentic human-created media.

Preferred examples:

> **AI-Generated Synthetic Media**

or, for a real-person avatar:

> **AI-generated avatar. The person depicted did not record this video.**

Platform-generated labels should not be the only disclosure for high-risk content.

---

## 8. Provenance and Records

Where technically supported, the organization should preserve provenance or Content Credentials.

Each Tier 2 and Tier 3 project record should retain:

- original source;
- final published file;
- generation tool;
- creation date;
- significant editing steps;
- consent record;
- approval record;
- disclosure method;
- distribution channels.

---

## 9. Human Review

The reviewer must confirm:

- factual accuracy;
- consent;
- disclosure;
- audience and context;
- vendor approval;
- compliance with prohibited uses.

Tier 3 projects require a second reviewer.

---

## 10. Refusal

The organization must refuse a request when:

- consent is missing or unclear;
- the request creates false attribution;
- the message is materially consequential and synthetic representation would create undue authority or confusion;
- required disclosure cannot be maintained;
- the requester is attempting to bypass governance;
- responsibility for the final content cannot be assigned.

---

## 11. Incident Response

When synthetic media is misused or materially incorrect:

1. Pause distribution.
2. Preserve the original and relevant records.
3. Determine affected channels and audiences.
4. Notify the responsible communications and legal/privacy stakeholders.
5. Correct or withdraw the communication when necessary.
6. Document the root cause and update controls.

---

## 12. Accountability

The AI platform is not the accountable communicator.

The project owner is responsible for the purpose, source content, and workflow. The approving communications leader is responsible for the approval decision. The organization remains responsible for public communications it distributes.

---

## 13. Industry-Specific High-Risk Areas

The following corporate uses require heightened review or refusal:

- financial reporting or investor communications;
- earnings and market-sensitive announcements;
- executive statements;
- employment and disciplinary communications;
- customer safety or product-recall communications;
- regulatory or legal notices;
- crisis communications;
- representations that could affect a person's employment or reputation.

For these areas, an authentic human spokesperson should generally be preferred when personal attribution is material to the message.
